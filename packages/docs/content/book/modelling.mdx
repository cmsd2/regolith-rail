## What you will learn

- To formulate a decision as a model with decision variables, parameters, constraints and an objective
  function, and to identify its feasible and optimal solutions.
- To formulate a recurring decision as a dynamic system with a state equation, and a policy as the rule
  that selects each action.
- To compute the capacity of a line and the steady state of a policy.
- To prove that a policy cannot meet a given demand, and to compute how that conclusion depends on the
  parameters.

## The problem

Operations research studies decisions through models. A model describes an operation in simplified form.
It includes the quantities a decision depends on and omits everything else. The omissions cost some
accuracy, and in return the model can be solved and studied (Eiselt and Sandblom, 2022, pp. 4–5). The best
solution to a model is the best plan for the operation only when the model represents the operation well
(Taha, 2017, p. 34).

This chapter models a freight line with two stations. A mine at one end produces a resource, a habitat
dome at the other end consumes it, and one train carries it between them. At each stop the train decides
how much to load or unload. The chapter first formulates a single such decision as an optimisation
problem. It then formulates the sequence of decisions as a dynamic system governed by a policy, and
compares two policies through their steady states.

## The model

### A single decision

<Definition id="def-model" name="Model">

A **model** of a decision consists of

- **decision variables**, the quantities the decision-maker chooses;
- **parameters**, the quantities that are given;
- **constraints**, conditions on the decision variables that every admissible choice satisfies;
- an **objective function** of the decision variables, to be maximised or minimised.

</Definition>

<Definition id="def-feasible" name="Feasible and optimal solutions">

A choice of values for the decision variables is **feasible** if it satisfies every constraint. A feasible
choice is **optimal** if no feasible choice gives the objective function a better value. An inequality
constraint is **binding** at a solution if it holds there with equality, and otherwise has **slack**.

</Definition>

These definitions follow Eiselt and Sandblom (2022, pp. 5–6) and Taha (2017, pp. 33–34, 119).

Consider one stop. The train stands at the mine with no cargo and chooses the amount $a$ to load. The
mine's station has $m \ge 0$ units in stock. The train can carry at most $\kappa$ units. The dome's
station has capacity $\bar x$ and will have $d \le \bar x$ units in stock when the train arrives there.
Loading as much as the dome can accept is the problem

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximise}\quad & a \\
\text{subject to}\quad & a \le m, \quad a \le \kappa, \quad a \le \bar x - d, \quad a \ge 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $a$ is the decision variable and $m$, $\kappa$, $\bar x$ and $d$ are parameters.

<Proposition id="prop-one-stop">

The problem has the unique optimal solution $a^* = \min(m, \kappa, \bar x - d)$.

</Proposition>

<Proof>

The four constraints hold together if and only if $0 \le a \le \min(m, \kappa, \bar x - d)$. Since
$m \ge 0$ and $d \le \bar x$, and the train's capacity $\kappa$ is positive, this interval is non-empty,
so feasible solutions exist. The objective function increases strictly with $a$, so its maximum over the interval is attained at
the right endpoint and nowhere else.

</Proof>

At $a^*$ at least one of the three upper constraints is binding, and the proof shows why: an optimal
solution with slack in all three could be increased.

<WorkedExample id="ex-one-stop">

Let $m = 20$, $\kappa = 30$, $\bar x = 30$ and $d = 12$. Then $a^* = \min(20, 30, 18) = 18$. The
constraint $a \le \bar x - d$ is binding. The constraints $a \le m$ and $a \le \kappa$ have slack 2 and 12.

</WorkedExample>

<Check ref="maxima:one-stop" />

<Remark id="rem-proxy">

The operator's aims are that the dome never runs short and that the mine never stops for lack of room.
The objective "load as much as the dome can accept" stands in for those aims. Such a **proxy objective**
can be measured and optimised (Eiselt and Sandblom, 2022, p. 5). It takes no account of the stops that
follow, which the next section brings into the model.

</Remark>

### Repeated decisions

The train returns to the dome once in every round trip, and the decision recurs. A model of the recurring
decision describes how the stock changes from one decision to the next.

<Definition id="def-state-equation" name="State equation">

Number the train's arrivals at the dome $k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ Let $x_k$ be the dome's stock at arrival
$k$, let $a_k$ be the amount the train unloads there, and let $\xi_k \ge 0$ be the demand between arrival
$k$ and arrival $k + 1$. Demand that finds no stock is lost. Write $(y)^+ = \max(y, 0)$. The stock at
the next arrival and the demand that goes unmet in between are

$$
x_{k+1} = (x_k + a_k - \xi_k)^+, \qquad w_k = (\xi_k - x_k - a_k)^+ .
$$

The first equation is the **state equation**. The stock $x_k$ is the **state**, the amount $a_k$ is the
**action**, and the demand $\xi_k$ is a **disturbance**, a quantity the operator does not choose. The
action satisfies $0 \le a_k \le \bar x - x_k$ and cannot exceed the cargo aboard.

</Definition>

Hernández-Lerma et al. (2023, p. 4) write a production and inventory system in this form.

<Lemma id="lem-conservation" name="Conservation">

For every $k$,

$$
x_{k+1} + (\xi_k - w_k) = x_k + a_k .
$$

The stock after the period and the demand met during it together equal the stock before the period and
the delivery.

</Lemma>

<Proof>

Every real number $y$ satisfies $(y)^+ - (-y)^+ = y$. Apply this with $y = x_k + a_k - \xi_k$. Then
$(y)^+ = x_{k+1}$ and $(-y)^+ = w_k$, so $x_{k+1} - w_k = x_k + a_k - \xi_k$.

</Proof>

  maxima:state-equation

" />

<Definition id="def-policy" name="Policy">

A **policy** $\pi$ is a rule that determines each action from the quantities observed when the action is
taken. A policy that uses the current state is a **feedback** policy.

</Definition>

The term feedback policy, also closed-loop policy, is that of Hernández-Lerma et al. (2023, p. 5). A single
decision has an objective function of the decision variable. A recurring decision has an objective that is
a function of the policy. This chapter uses the demand left unmet over $n$ round trips,
$J_n(\pi) = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} w_k$, and reports beside it the production that the mine loses while its
station is full.

### The two-station line

<Assumption id="asm-line" name="The line">

The line and its train have the following properties.

1. The line has two stations, the mine and the dome. Each stores at most $\bar x$ units of the resource.
2. The mine produces at the constant rate $v$ while its station has room, and produces nothing while its
   station is full.
3. The dome consumes at the constant rate $u$ while its station has stock. Demand that arrives while the
   station is empty is lost.
4. One train of capacity $\kappa$ travels back and forth between the stations. A leg takes time $\tau$.
5. On arriving at a station the train loads or unloads an amount $q$ at once, and departs after a
   further time $t_0 + t_1 q$.
6. The train's policy determines $q$ on arrival from the stock at both stations and the cargo aboard.

</Assumption>

The chapter's examples and exercises use the following values, with time in hours.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time for one leg | $\tau$ | 4 hours |
| Time for a stop, before moving anything | $t_0$ | 10 minutes, $1/6$ hour |
| Extra time for a stop, for each unit moved | $t_1$ | 1 minute, $1/60$ hour |
| The train's capacity | $\kappa$ | 30 units |
| Each station's capacity | $\bar x$ | 30 units |
| The mine's production rate | $v$ | 45 units a day, $15/8$ an hour |
| The dome's consumption rate | $u$ | 36 units a day, $3/2$ an hour |

The stations are 72,000 distance units apart and the train travels 5 units a second, which gives
$\tau = 4$ hours.

### Capacity

<Definition id="def-cycle" name="Cycle">

A **cycle** with load $a$ is a round trip in which the train loads $a$ units at the mine and unloads $a$
units at the dome. Its duration and its **throughput**, the amount delivered per unit time, are

$$
T(a) = 2\tau + 2(t_0 + t_1 a), \qquad f(a) = \frac{a}{T(a)} .
$$

The **capacity** of the line is $f(\kappa)$, the throughput of a cycle with a full load.

</Definition>

<Proposition id="prop-capacity" name="Capacity of the line">

The throughput $f(a)$ is strictly increasing in $a \ge 0$. A load cannot exceed $\kappa$, so no cycle has
a throughput above the capacity.

</Proposition>

<Proof>

Differentiating gives

$$
f'(a) = \frac{T(a) - 2 t_1 a}{T(a)^2} = \frac{2(\tau + t_0)}{T(a)^2} > 0 .
$$

</Proof>

Each unit added to the load lengthens the cycle by $2 t_1$, while the fixed part $2(\tau + t_0)$ of the
cycle is shared among more units.

<WorkedExample id="ex-capacity">

For the line of <Ref to="asm-line" />, $T(a) = 25/3 + a/30$ hours. A full load gives

$$
T(30) = \frac{28}{3}\ \text{hours}, \qquad f(30) = \frac{45}{14}\ \text{units an hour} = \frac{540}{7}
\approx 77.1\ \text{units a day}.
$$

The demand of 36 units a day is less than half of this capacity.

</WorkedExample>

<Check refs="
  maxima:cycle-time
  maxima:throughput
  maxima:capacity-bound
" />

<Remark id="rem-every-policy">

A demand above the capacity cannot be met by any cycle. Chapter 2 proves two statements about the line
that hold for every policy. No policy delivers more than the capacity (<Ref to="prop-capacity-bound" />).
The mine produces $v - u = 9$ units a day more than the dome consumes, and no policy can deliver that
surplus, so over a long period the mine loses at least 9 units a day of production
(<Ref to="cor-throughput" />).

</Remark>

### Steady states

<Definition id="def-steady-state" name="Steady state">

The line is in a **steady state** under a policy if every round trip repeats the one before: at each
arrival at a given station, the stock at both stations and the cargo aboard equal their values at the
previous arrival there.

</Definition>

<Proposition id="prop-needed-load" name="Load that meets demand">

Suppose $2 u t_1 < 1$. Consider a steady state in which the train loads only at the mine and unloads only
at the dome, and in which no demand is unmet. Then every round trip is a cycle with load

$$
\bar a := \frac{2u(\tau + t_0)}{1 - 2 u t_1}, \qquad\text{and}\qquad T(\bar a) = \frac{\bar a}{u} .
$$

</Proposition>

<Proof>

The cargo aboard is the same at successive arrivals at the dome, so the train unloads at the dome the
amount $a$ that it loads at the mine, and every round trip is a cycle with load $a$. The dome's stock
satisfies $x_{k+1} = x_k$, so <Ref to="lem-conservation" /> gives $a = \xi_k - w_k$: the amount delivered
in a round trip equals the demand met during it. No demand is unmet, so $w_k = 0$ and
$\xi_k = u\,T(a)$. Hence $a = u\,T(a) = 2u(\tau + t_0) + 2 u t_1 a$. Solving for $a$ gives $\bar a$, and
$T(\bar a) = \bar a / u$.

</Proof>

<WorkedExample id="ex-needed-load">

For the line, $2 u t_1 = 1/20$ and

$$
\bar a = \frac{2 \times \tfrac32 \times \tfrac{25}{6}}{1 - \tfrac{1}{20}} = \frac{250}{19} \approx 13.16\
\text{units}, \qquad T(\bar a) = \frac{500}{57} \approx 8.77\ \text{hours}.
$$

</WorkedExample>

<Check refs="
  maxima:needed-load-formula
  maxima:needed-load
" />

The condition $2 u t_1 < 1$ states that the train's two stops handle a unit in less time than the dome
takes to consume one. The load $\bar a$ is the load whose cycle has throughput $f(\bar a) = u$. Since
$f$ is increasing, $\bar a \le \kappa$ exactly when $u \le f(\kappa)$, that is, when the demand is within
the capacity.

### Two policies

<Definition id="def-drain-fill" name="Drain-and-fill policy">

On arriving at the mine with cargo $c$, the train loads $\min(m, \kappa - c)$, where $m$ is the mine's
stock. On arriving at the dome it unloads $\min(c, \bar x - d)$, where $d$ is the dome's stock.

</Definition>

<Definition id="def-balancing" name="Balancing policy">

On arriving at a station, the train moves that station's stock towards the mean of the two stations'
stocks, as far as its cargo $c$ and its capacity $\kappa$ allow. Let $s$ be the stock at the station where the
train stands and $s'$ the stock at the other. If $s > s'$ the train loads
$\min\big((s - s')/2,\ \kappa - c\big)$. If $s < s'$ it unloads $\min\big((s' - s)/2,\ c\big)$.

</Definition>

<Check ref="maxima:half-difference" />

The amount $(s - s')/2$ is the difference between the station's stock and the mean $(s + s')/2$.
Drain-and-fill uses the knowledge that the mine supplies and the dome consumes. Balancing treats the
stations alike and needs no such knowledge. Both are feedback policies.

<Proposition id="prop-drain-fill" name="Drain-and-fill meets demand">

Suppose $v \ge u$ and $\bar a \le \min(\kappa, \bar x)$. Then under drain-and-fill the line has a steady
state with load $\bar a$ in which no demand is unmet.

</Proposition>

<Proof>

The proof exhibits the steady state. Let the dome's station be full after a stop, and let the next stop
follow after a time $T(\bar a)$. In that time the dome consumes $u\,T(\bar a) = \bar a \le \bar x$ units,
so it does not run out, and at the next stop it has room for exactly $\bar a$ units. If the train then has
at least $\bar a$ units aboard, it unloads $\bar a$ and the station is full again. It remains to show that
in each round trip the train loads $\bar a$ at the mine and reaches the dome with at least $\bar a$
aboard.

If $v = u$, the mine produces $\bar a$ units in each round trip. The train drains them, unloads them all
at the dome and returns empty.

If $v > u$, the mine produces more than $\bar a$ in each round trip, so its station is full at each
arrival. The train leaves the mine with a full load $\kappa$, unloads $\bar a$, returns with
$\kappa - \bar a$ aboard and loads $\bar a$ again.

</Proof>

<Check ref="maxima:drain-fill-steady" />

The corresponding statement for balancing is a necessary condition on the stations' capacity.

<Proposition id="prop-balancing-capacity" name="Capacity that balancing requires">

Suppose that under balancing the line is in a steady state in which no demand is unmet and the train
arrives at the mine empty. Then

$$
\bar x \ \ge\ \bar x_{\min} := 2\bar a + u(\tau + t_0 + t_1 \bar a) .
$$

</Proposition>

<Proof>

The train arrives at the mine empty, so it unloads nothing there and loaded nothing at the dome. By
<Ref to="prop-needed-load" /> every round trip is a cycle with load $\bar a$. Let $m$ and $d$ be the
stocks at the mine and the dome when the train arrives at the mine. By <Ref to="def-balancing" /> the
train loads $\min\big((m - d)/2,\ \kappa\big) = \bar a$, which requires $(m - d)/2 \ge \bar a$, that is,
$m \ge d + 2\bar a$.

The train reaches the dome after a further time $t_0 + t_1 \bar a + \tau$. The dome receives nothing in
that interval and no demand is unmet, so $d \ge u(\tau + t_0 + t_1 \bar a)$.

The mine's stock cannot exceed its capacity, so
$\bar x \ge m \ge d + 2\bar a \ge 2\bar a + u(\tau + t_0 + t_1 \bar a)$.

</Proof>

<WorkedExample id="ex-balancing-capacity">

For the line,

$$
\bar x_{\min} = 2 \times \frac{250}{19} + \frac32\left(4 + \frac16 + \frac{1}{60} \times \frac{250}{19}\right)
= \frac{500}{19} + \frac{125}{19} = \frac{625}{19} \approx 32.89\ \text{units}.
$$

The stations' capacity is 30. Under balancing the line therefore has no steady state in which the train
arrives at the mine empty and no demand is unmet, although the line's capacity is more than twice the
demand.

</WorkedExample>

<Check refs="
  maxima:capacity-requirement
  maxima:smallest-station
" />

The proof identifies two requirements that compete for the stations' capacity. Balancing loads half the
difference in stock, so a load of $\bar a$ needs a difference of $2\bar a$. The dome must also have enough
stock at that moment to last until the train reaches it.

The next result describes a steady state under balancing in which demand is unmet. The example that
follows verifies that its conditions are consistent on the line of <Ref to="asm-line" />, and the
simulator section shows that runs of the line settle into it.

<Proposition id="prop-balancing-steady" name="Steady state under balancing">

Suppose that under balancing the line is in a steady state in which

1. the mine's station is full at each arrival at the mine,
2. the dome has stock at each arrival at the mine and none at each arrival at the dome, and
3. the train unloads its whole cargo at the dome.

Then the load is

$$
a_B = \frac{\bar x + u(\tau + t_0)}{3 - u t_1},
$$

and demand goes unmet at the average rate $u - f(a_B)$.

</Proposition>

<Proof>

Let $d$ be the dome's stock when the train arrives at the mine. By (1) and (3) the mine's stock is
$\bar x$ and the train is empty, so it loads $a_B = (\bar x - d)/2$.

By (2) and (3) the dome's stock was $a_B$ just after the previous delivery. Since then the train has
stopped at the dome and travelled one leg, a time $t_0 + t_1 a_B + \tau$, and by (2) the dome had stock
throughout. Hence $d = a_B - u(\tau + t_0 + t_1 a_B)$.

Substituting the second equation into the first gives $2 a_B = \bar x - a_B + u(\tau + t_0) + u t_1 a_B$,
which is the formula for $a_B$. Each round trip lasts $T(a_B)$ and delivers $a_B$ against a demand of
$u\,T(a_B)$, so the unmet demand per unit time is $u - a_B / T(a_B) = u - f(a_B)$.

</Proof>

<WorkedExample id="ex-balancing-steady">

For the line,

$$
a_B = \frac{30 + \tfrac32 \times \tfrac{25}{6}}{3 - \tfrac{1}{40}} = \frac{1450}{119} \approx 12.18\
\text{units}, \qquad T(a_B) = \frac{1040}{119} \approx 8.74\ \text{hours}.
$$

The three conditions are consistent with these values. When the train arrives at the mine the dome has
$d \approx 5.63$ units, which last 3.75 hours, and the train arrives 4.37 hours later. The mine produces
16.4 units during a round trip and the train removes 12.18, so the mine's station refills. At the dome,
half the difference in stock exceeds the cargo, so the train unloads all of it. The shortfall is

$$
u - f(a_B) = \frac32 - \frac{145}{104} = \frac{11}{104}\ \text{units an hour} = \frac{33}{13} \approx 2.54\
\text{units a day},
$$

and the mine loses $v - f(a_B) = 150/13 \approx 11.54$ units a day of production.

</WorkedExample>

## In the simulator

The simulator runs the line of <Ref to="asm-line" /> as the `two-station` starter scenario, for 10 days.
Its policies are written with the `ops` library. A policy examines each *site*, a station together with a
resource stored there. It may assign the site a role, and it sets a target for the site's stock. The train
then loads or unloads the difference between the stock and the target, as far as its cargo and the
available room allow.

<Scenario starter="two-station" />

The drain-and-fill policy of <Ref to="def-drain-fill" /> assigns roles by station. `ops.drain {}` sets a
target of zero and `ops.fill {}` a target of the station's capacity:

```lua runnable scenario=two-station fix
return ops.policy {
  classify = ops.roles.manual { Mine = "supply", Dome = "demand" },
  target = { supply = ops.drain {}, demand = ops.fill {} },
}
```

The balancing policy of <Ref to="def-balancing" /> is the built-in `balance-stock`, the scenario's
baseline. `ops.balance {}` sets every site's target to the mean stock of the stations that store the
resource:

```lua
return ops.policy { target = ops.balance {} }
```

The starter's dome consumes at a rate that varies. A run draws its random numbers from a **seed**, and the
same seed always gives the same run. The following steps reproduce the model, in which the rate is
constant.

1. Open the drain-and-fill policy above. The Scenario tab shows the line as a scenario script.
2. In the script, change the dome's `variability = 20` to `variability = 0`, and run. The stop inspector,
   opened from a stop marker on the timeline, shows each load.
3. Put <PolicyLink policy="balance-stock">`balance-stock`</PolicyLink> in the Policy slot, which keeps the
   edited scenario, and run again.
4. For the rate of unmet demand in the steady state, compare the unmet demand of runs with
   `duration = sols(5)` and `duration = sols(20)`.

## Case study: half capacity

The `two-station` starter illustrates the failure that this project calls half capacity: under balancing
the line delivers less than the dome consumes, although the train could carry more than twice the demand.
<Ref to="prop-balancing-capacity" /> gives the reason, and its bound has a simple form.

<Corollary id="cor-capacity-factor">

The capacity of <Ref to="prop-balancing-capacity" /> satisfies $\bar x_{\min} = \tfrac52 \bar a$.
Consequently balancing can meet a demand $u$ in such a steady state only if $u \le f(2\bar x / 5)$.

</Corollary>

<Proof>

From <Ref to="prop-needed-load" />, $u(\tau + t_0) = \bar a\,(1 - 2 u t_1)/2$. Hence

$$
\bar x_{\min} = 2\bar a + u(\tau + t_0) + u t_1 \bar a
= 2\bar a + \frac{\bar a}{2} - u t_1 \bar a + u t_1 \bar a = \frac52\,\bar a .
$$

The condition $\bar x \ge \bar x_{\min}$ is therefore $\bar a \le 2\bar x / 5$. Since $f$ is increasing
and $f(\bar a) = u$, this is the condition $u \le f(2\bar x / 5)$.

</Proof>

<WorkedExample id="ex-half-capacity">

With $\bar x = 30$, balancing can sustain a load of at most 12 units, and

$$
f(12) = \frac{180}{131}\ \text{units an hour} = \frac{4320}{131} \approx 32.98\ \text{units a day}.
$$

This is 43% of the line's capacity of 77.1 units a day, and less than the demand of 36. Drain-and-fill
requires only $\bar a \le \min(\kappa, \bar x)$ and can meet any demand up to the line's capacity.

</WorkedExample>

<Check refs="
  maxima:capacity-factor
  maxima:balancing-max-demand
" />

The corollary also gives the **sensitivity** of the conclusion to the parameters, the third question of
a study after feasibility and optimality (Eiselt and Sandblom, 2022, p. 7). The load $\bar a$ increases
with the demand $u$ and with the leg time $\tau$, and $\bar x_{\min}$ increases in proportion. A shorter
line or a larger station can satisfy the condition, and a higher demand moves the line further from it.
Exercises 2 to 4 compute three such cases.

## Where the simulator differs

- **Discrete time and stock.** The simulator advances in steps of one minute and moves stock in
  thousandths of a unit. Its loads under balancing settle at 12.18 where the model gives $1450/119
  \approx 12.185$.
- **Variable demand.** With `variability = 20` the dome's rate is drawn every two hours, uniformly within
  20% of 36 units a day. The model assumes a constant rate.
- **Conservation.** The simulator maintains the account of <Ref to="lem-conservation" /> at every event:
  stock and cargo stay between zero and capacity, and the total of each resource balances.

  <Check ref="test:conserves resources and keeps stock and cargo within bounds at every event" />
- **Balancing as an assumption.** The baseline implements <Ref to="def-balancing" />.
  [Game mechanics](/docs/game-mechanics#station-balancing) records how closely that matches the game, with
  the evidence.

   - the unmet demand in units a day, $24\,(u - f(a_B))$.

<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar a &= \frac{2 \times 1.8 \times (4 + \tfrac16)}{1 - 2 \times 1.8 \times \tfrac{1}{60}} = \frac{750}{47} \approx 15.96, \\
\bar x_{\min} &= \tfrac52\,\bar a \approx 39.89, \\
a_B &= \frac{30 + 1.8\,(4 + \tfrac16)}{3 - \tfrac{1.8}{60}} = \frac{37.5}{2.97} \approx 12.63, \qquad
T(a_B) = 8 + 2\,(\tfrac16 + \tfrac{a_B}{60}) \approx 8.75\ \text{hours}, \\
24\,(u - f(a_B)) &\approx 43.2 - 34.62 = 8.58\ \text{units a day}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<GameNote>

In the rail game, trains appear to balance stock across the stations on a line, the policy of
<Ref to="def-balancing" />. A game must work for every line a player builds, and a policy that needs no
roles is a reasonable default. A mine at one end of a long line and a dome at the other is the case in
which it costs most: the dome runs short and the mine's extractors stop, although the train could carry
far more.
