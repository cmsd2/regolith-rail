Symbols keep one meaning throughout the book, with these exceptions, which the tables note. $L$ is the
mean number present in Little's law and the lead time elsewhere. $D$ is a random demand, except in
chapter 4, where it is a constant demand rate. $S$ is the total stock on a line in chapter 2 and an
order-up-to level in Part II. An indexed $a_i$ is an arrival time in chapter 2, $a$ without an index is a
load in chapter 1, and $a$ is the level of a demand model in chapter 8. $\alpha$ is a service target in
chapter 7 and a smoothing constant in chapter 8. $\tau$ is the time of one leg in Part I, and in chapter 6
the time since an order arrived. A symbol used only inside one proof or example, such as the stock at a
named station, is defined where it appears and omitted here.

Numbered statements are cited as, for example, Theorem 2.11: the chapter, then the statement's place in
the chapter. Rates are per hour in Part I unless a day is stated, and per day in Part II. Costs are in
whole cost units.
