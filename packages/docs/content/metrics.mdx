Every run reports the metrics below. Quantities are shown in units; the engine counts
milli-units. Batches compare the same metrics across seeds.

Consumption and production happen once per game minute. In each minute $t$, a consumer
$c$ wants $d_{c,t}$ from its station, which holds $s_{c,t}$; a producer $p$ makes
$m_{p,t}$ into a station with room $r_{p,t}$.

## Unmet demand (weighted)

Unmet demand for each resource, multiplied by that resource's priority from the
scenario. **Lower is better.** This is the headline number: it scores a food shortage
above a metal shortage when food has the higher priority.

$$
U_w = \sum_{\text{resources } k} \text{priority}_k \cdot U_k
$$

## Unmet demand

What consumers wanted but could not take, because their station ran out. **Lower is
better.**

$$
U = \sum_t \sum_c \max\left(0,\; d_{c,t} - s_{c,t}\right)
$$

## Stalled production

What producers made but could not store, because their station was full. **Lower is
better.** In the game this is the moment extractors and factories stop working.

$$
S = \sum_t \sum_p \max\left(0,\; m_{p,t} - r_{p,t}\right)
$$

## Demand met

What consumers took from their stations. **Higher is better**, but compare it with
unmet demand: a scenario with more demand meets more.

## Empty distance share

The share of distance trains travelled with no cargo at all. **Lower is better.** Some
empty running is unavoidable on a shuttle line, where a train must return from a
demand station to fetch more.

$$
E = \frac{\text{distance travelled empty}}{\text{distance travelled}}
$$

It is 0 when no train has moved.

## Dwell time

Total time trains spent stopped. Each stop takes the train's fixed dwell time plus its
dwell per unit for every whole unit moved. **Lower is better** for the same deliveries.

## Oscillations

The number of loads at a station, for a resource, within one round trip of the last
unload of that resource there. A round trip is the time the loading vehicle takes to go
once round its route: out and back for a shuttle, one circuit for a loop, one trip for a
timetable. **Lower is better.** High counts mean stock is being
carried back the way it came; see [ping-pong](/docs/failure-modes/ping-pong).

## Stops

The number of times a train stopped at a station. It depends mostly on the scenario
and on dwell time.

## Transferred

Total amount loaded and unloaded. Moving more is not better in itself: ping-pong moves
a lot and delivers little.

## Warnings

The number of actions the engine clamped, such as loading more than a station holds,
and recorded values it ignored because they were not finite. **Lower is better**;
clamping usually means the policy's picture of the line is wrong. The stop inspector
shows what was requested and what was applied.

## Policy errors

Stops where the policy raised an error, including budget overruns. The train leaves
without transfers after an error. **Lower is better**; zero is the goal.

## Budget overruns

Stops where the policy ran past the [instruction budget](/docs/language#instruction-budget).
These are also counted as policy errors.

## Backorders (average)

Demand waiting to be served, averaged over every minute of the run. Only consumers that
backorder contribute; other consumers lose unmet demand instead. **Lower is better.**
Shown only for scenarios with backordering consumers.

## Backorders (peak)

The largest total backorder at the end of any minute. **Lower is better.**

## Expired stock

Stock removed at reviews because it expires, as in the newsvendor problem. **Lower is
better.** Shown only for scenarios with expiring stock.

## Delivery overflow

Deliveries from suppliers that did not fit where they arrived. **Lower is better.** Shown
only for scenarios with suppliers.

## Converters starved

Time, summed over converters, when a batch was due but its inputs were not in stock.
**Lower is better.** Shown only for scenarios with converters.

## Converters blocked

Time, summed over converters, when a batch was due but there was no room for its outputs.
**Lower is better.**

## Total cost

The sum of every cost the scenario states, in cost units. Each component below is rounded
down to a thousandth of a unit. **Lower is better.** Shown only for scenarios that state
costs.

## Holding cost

Cost per unit of stock held per sol, charged every minute.

## Ordering cost

A fixed cost for each order plus a cost for each unit ordered.

## Transport cost

Cost per unit of distance each vehicle travels.

## Lost demand cost

Cost per unit of demand that could not be met and was lost.

## Backorder cost

Cost per unit of backordered demand per sol, charged every minute.

## Stalled production cost

Cost per unit of production that did not fit at its station.

## Comparing policies

A batch runs both policies on the same seeds, so each seed gives a paired difference
$\Delta_i = b_i - a_i$. The batch view reports the mean difference with a 95%
confidence interval from Student's t distribution:

$$
\bar{\Delta} \pm t_{0.975,\,n-1} \cdot \frac{s_\Delta}{\sqrt{n}}
$$

A difference is called better or worse only when the interval leaves out zero;
otherwise it is within noise. More seeds narrow the interval.
