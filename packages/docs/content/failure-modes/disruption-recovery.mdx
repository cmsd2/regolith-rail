Scenario: **Storm shock**. The two-station line with a dust storm that arrives after three sols.

## What happens

The storm disrupts the line twice. For one sol it stops the mine's extractors: a supply
shock. Half a sol after it starts, repairs to storm damage send the dome's metal use up
to two and a half times normal for a sol and a half: a demand shock. The dome's station
empties quickly, and shortages carry on well after the storm has passed.

## Why

Under balancing, the dome's station already hovers near empty in normal operation (see
[half capacity](/docs/failure-modes/half-capacity)). It holds no safety stock to draw on
when supply stops, and when demand jumps there is no stock to meet it. Balancing does not
react to the disruption either: it keeps aiming for the line average, which falls as the
mine's stock is used up.

Storms are *states of the world*: while a storm is active, rates change. The timeline
shows each event as a band, so you can see what your policy did during it.

## What helps

Safety stock is the simplest way to make a line resilient: a buffer at the demand end,
kept topped up in normal operation, absorbs the first part of a shock and shortens the
recovery. [ops.min_max](/docs/ops/min-max) refills a site when it falls below a minimum:

```lua runnable scenario=storm-shock
return ops.policy {
  classify = ops.roles.manual { Mine = "supply", Dome = "demand" },
  target = {
    supply = ops.drain {},
    demand = ops.min_max { min = 20000, max = 30000 },
  },
}
```

Compare recovery with a batch: [unmet demand](/docs/metrics#unmet-demand) over many
seeds shows how much of the storm each policy rides out.
