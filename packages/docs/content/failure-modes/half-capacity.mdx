Scenario: **Two stations**. A mine's station and a dome's station at either end of a
long line, one train, metals only.

## What happens

The mine produces more metal than the dome consumes, and the train could carry enough
on each trip. Yet under the naive baseline the dome's station runs empty between
visits while the mine's station fills up and its extractors stall.

## Why

Balancing moves each station towards the average stock across the line. With two
stations, the average sits halfway between them, so a visit to the mine loads only
half the difference between the two stations, however much room the train has.

By the time the train reaches the dome, the dome has consumed part of its stock, and
the train unloads down to a new average. Each round trip carries roughly half of what
it could, and the gap that builds between trips is never closed.

Two metrics show it: [unmet demand](/docs/metrics#unmet-demand) at the dome and
[stalled production](/docs/metrics#stalled-production) at the mine, both at once.

## What helps

Treat the stations by role instead of by average: take everything from the supply
end and leave everything at the demand end.

```lua runnable
return ops.policy {
  classify = ops.roles.manual { Mine = "supply", Dome = "demand" },
  target = { supply = ops.drain {}, demand = ops.fill {} },
}
```

Run a batch comparing this with `naive.lua` to see how much of the shortage it removes,
and what it costs in [empty distance](/docs/metrics#empty-distance-share).
