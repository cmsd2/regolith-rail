## The workbench

The [workbench](/) opens with the *Two stations* scenario and `naive`, a policy that
imitates how trains in the game appear to balance stock. The library is on the left,
with **This run** showing the scenario and policy in use. The editor is in the middle,
and the line map, timeline and results are on the right.

## Run the baseline

Press **Run**. The simulation plays ten sols of game time in a moment, then the line
map replays it. Drag along the timeline, or press play, to watch stock move. Select a
stop marker to see what the policy saw and what it did in the stop inspector.

Open the **Metrics** tab. The number to watch is
[unmet demand (weighted)](/docs/metrics#unmet-demand-weighted): how much the dome
wanted to consume but could not, because its station was empty.

## Write a policy

Replace the editor contents with a policy built from [ops blocks](/docs/ops). The
built-in `naive` policy never changes: your edits go to a copy under **Mine** in the
library, which saves itself as you type.

```lua runnable
return ops.policy {
  classify = ops.roles.manual { Mine = "supply", Dome = "demand" },
  target = {
    supply = ops.drain {},
    demand = ops.fill {},
  },
}
```

The mine's station is a supply site, so trains take everything they can; the dome's
station is a demand site, so trains leave everything they carry. Press **Run** again
and compare the metrics.

Policies can also be plain Lua. A policy is a table with an `on_stop` function, and
optionally `on_start`. Each receives a context, `ctx`, where `ctx.here` is the station
the vehicle stopped at and `ctx.vehicle` is the vehicle:

```lua runnable
local policy = {}

function policy.on_start(ctx)
  ctx.log("stations:", #ctx.station_order)
end

function policy.on_stop(ctx)
  if ctx.here.id == "Mine" then
    ctx.load("Metals", ctx.here.stock.Metals)
  else
    ctx.unload("Metals", ctx.vehicle.cargo.Metals)
  end
end

return policy
```

```text output
stations: 2
```

Amounts are in milli-units: 1 unit of metal is `1000`. Requests the train or station
cannot satisfy are clamped and counted as [warnings](/docs/metrics#warnings).

## Compare over many seeds

One run is one roll of the dice. Switch to **Batch** and tick **Compare**. This run
gains a **Compare** slot: press **Choose…** beside it and double-click a second policy,
such as `naive`, then press **Run**. Each metric shows its distribution for both policies
and the paired difference with a 95% confidence interval. A difference is only called
better or worse when the interval leaves out zero.

## Share it

**Save run…** keeps the scenario, policies, seed and view together under **Saved runs**.

**Share link** makes a link holding your policies, scenario and settings. Nothing is
uploaded: the contents live in the part of the link after `#`. Whoever opens it finds
the run under **Shared with me**, and sees your code before anything runs.

[Library and saved runs](/docs/library) covers the library in full, including import
and export.
