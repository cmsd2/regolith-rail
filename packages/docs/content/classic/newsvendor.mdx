## The problem

A newsstand orders papers once a day, before it knows how many customers will come.
Papers left at the end of the day are worthless; customers who find none go
elsewhere. Ordering too many wastes what they cost, and ordering too few loses the
sale. The question is how many to order.

It is the simplest problem in which uncertainty shapes the best decision, and the
reasoning behind it, balancing the cost of too much against the cost of too little,
reappears in almost every inventory policy (Axsäter, 2015, pp. 95–97; Snyder and Shen,
2019, pp. 90–101).

## The scenario

One station, `Stand`, stores `Papers` with unlimited room.

- Demand for each day is drawn from a discrete distribution and spread evenly over the
  day. Demand that finds no stock is lost, at a cost of `lost_cost` per unit.
- Stock expires at each review, so nothing carries over.
- The stand is reviewed a minute into each day. It orders from an outside supplier
  with no lead time, at `unit_cost` per unit, and the papers arrive before any of that
  day's demand.

```lua runnable script
return classic.newsvendor {}
```

The defaults are demand of 5, 10, 15, 20 or 25 papers with weights 1, 2, 3, 2 and 1, a
unit cost of 2 and a lost-demand cost of 5, over 30 days.

## The reference result

With an order of $Q$ and demand $D$, a day costs

$$
C(Q) = cQ + p\,\mathbb{E}\left[(D - Q)^+\right]
$$

where $c$ is the unit cost and $p$ the lost-demand cost. Buying one more paper costs
$c$ for certain and saves $p$ only when demand exceeds $Q$, so the cost stops falling
at the smallest $Q$ with

$$
P(D \le Q) \ge \frac{p - c}{p}.
$$

This is the critical ratio. In textbook terms the underage cost is $c_u = p - c$, the
margin a lost sale gives up, and the overage cost is $c_o = c$, what an unsold paper
wastes, giving the familiar $c_u / (c_u + c_o)$ (Snyder and Shen, 2019, pp. 90–101;
Taha, 2017, pp. 618–620).

With the defaults the ratio is $3/5 = 0.6$. Demand is at most 15 with probability
$6/9 \approx 0.67$, the first value past 0.6, so the best order is 15 papers and the
expected cost is

$$
2 \times 15 + 5 \times \frac{5 \times 2 + 10 \times 1}{9} \approx 41.11 \text{ per day}.
$$

A test runs the template for 400 seeds and checks that 41.11 lies within the 99%
confidence interval of the simulated mean cost per day.

## The reference policy

Order up to the critical-ratio quantity at every review. After the leftovers expire
the stand holds nothing, so it orders exactly that quantity:

```lua runnable scenario=classic.newsvendor
return ops.policy { review = { target = ops.order_up_to { level = 15000 } } }
```

Try other levels with a batch comparison: costs rise on both sides of 15, faster below
it, because a lost sale costs more than an unsold paper.

## Where the simulator differs

- **Timing.** Demand arrives minute by minute rather than all at once. Only the total
  for the day matters to the cost, so the result is the same, but the stand runs out
  part way through a busy day rather than at its end.
- **Costs.** Costs are whole cost units per unit, so textbook examples with fractional
  prices need scaling.
- **Leftovers.** Unsold papers have no salvage value. They are counted in the
  [expired stock](/docs/metrics#expired-stock) metric rather than as a cost, since
  their cost was paid when they were ordered.
